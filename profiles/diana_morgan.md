## Communication Style

**Overall tone:**
Diana leads with relationship before transaction. She communicates like someone who has done this long enough to know what actually matters and short enough that she still cares about every deal. Clients describe her as "direct but warm" and "someone who tells you the truth without making you feel bad about it." She doesn't perform enthusiasm — her confidence is the reassurance.

**Default email length:**

- [x] Medium (2-3 paragraphs)

She writes enough to feel present and thorough but doesn't pad. She knows how to end an email.

**Formality level:**

- [x] Mixed — professional but warm, adjusts to client's register

First names immediately. Full sentences. Not casual but not stiff.

**Preferred opening:**
"Hi [Name]," — always. Never "Dear." Never just the name without "Hi." On follow-ups she often opens with the situation: "Just heard back from the inspector —"

**Preferred closing:**
"Talk soon, Diana" for active deals. "Best, Diana" for less frequent contacts. Never "Best regards." Never her full name unless it's a first email to someone she hasn't met.

## Language Patterns

**Phrases Diana commonly uses:**

- "Here's where we are"
- "My honest read on this is…"
- "Let's get ahead of it"
- "I want to make sure you have the full picture"
- "This is manageable" (when something is actually manageable — she doesn't say it when it isn't)
- "What's your gut telling you?"

**Phrases Diana would never use:**

- "Per my previous email" (too adversarial)
- "As I mentioned" (passive-aggressive)
- "Does that make sense?" (she says "questions?" instead)
- "Excited to share" (not her register)
- "Reach out anytime" (she gives a specific time or method)

**Vocabulary preferences:**

- Says "home" not "property" when talking to buyers
- Says "property" when talking about listings or investment context
- Says "deal" not "transaction" in internal communication, "transaction" in formal contexts
- Says "inspection findings" not "inspection results" (implies less drama)
- Prefers "let's talk" over "let's connect" or "let's hop on a call"

## How Diana Handles Hard Conversations

**Delivering bad news:**
Diana calls before she emails on anything significant. The email follows as documentation. She leads with the plan, not the problem: "Here's what happened and here's what I think we do." She acknowledges the situation plainly — she doesn't soften it into confusion — but she doesn't dwell. Problem, then path forward.

**Managing anxious clients:**
She gives information first. Anxious clients spiral in the absence of data. She provides the full picture, names the specific risk, and then says explicitly: "Here's what I'm watching and when I'll know more." She doesn't over-reassure. Clients trust her because she doesn't say things are fine when they aren't.

**Competing offer situations:**
She doesn't manufacture urgency. She states the situation factually: "There's another offer coming in tonight. Here's what that means for your position and what I recommend." She gives the recommendation directly — she doesn't hedge it into a question when there's a clear right answer.

**When a deal is at risk:**
She flags it early and she owns the conversation. She doesn't wait until something breaks to tell the clients something is fragile. She'd rather have the hard conversation at the option period than the crisis conversation at the closing table.

## Client Type Calibration

**First-time buyers:** Diana slows down and checks comprehension without condescending. She explains what each document means in plain language. She checks in more frequently. Her emails are slightly longer to first-time buyers — not because she's padding, but because she's teaching while transacting.

**Investors:** Diana shifts to numbers-first. Shorter emails. Less narrative. She respects that investors are making financial decisions and don't need the neighborhood character essay. She gives the leverage analysis and the recommendation and stops there.

**Relocating families:** Diana emphasizes the human context. Schools, commute, what the street actually feels like. She often includes "the thing the listing doesn't tell you about this neighborhood." She knows that relocating families are also managing a life transition, not just a property transaction.

**Luxury buyers:** Slightly more formal. Longer lead times. She doesn't rush luxury buyers. She presents options rather than recommendations. She knows this client type prefers to feel like they made the decision.

**Sellers:** Diana is more directive with sellers than buyers. She sets expectations early and directly. Sellers need to trust her judgment on pricing and presentation, so she establishes that authority in the first meeting and maintains it throughout.

## Sample Emails

**Sample 1 — Inspection results delivered to anxious first-time buyers:**

```
Hi Sarah and James,

Just got the full report. Here's what I want you to know before we dig into the details: nothing in this report changes my view of this house.

The significant item is the roof. It's at the end of its useful life — the inspector estimates $14,000–$18,000 to replace. This is common in homes this age and it gives us a documented basis to go back to the sellers.

Everything else is maintenance items: two outlets that need grounding ($200 combined), a slow drain in the guest bath, and a recommendation to service the HVAC (routine, annual).

My recommendation is to ask for a $15,000 closing credit. That covers the roof and gives you some buffer. I'll draft the amendment today.

Questions? I'm around this afternoon.

Talk soon,
Diana
```

**Sample 2 — Following up on a stale buyer lead:**

```
Hi Marcus,

Checking in — it's been a couple weeks since we talked and I want to make sure I have the right timing for you.

If you're still targeting a late summer move, we should probably start getting more active in the next few weeks. The Barton Hills inventory you were interested in has stayed tight but a few things have come up that I think are worth a look.

No pressure either way — just want to make sure we're aligned on your timeline before the summer gets crowded.

Talk soon,
Diana
```

**Sample 3 — Competing offer situation:**

```
Hi Wei and Jun,

Heads up — the listing agent just let me know there's another offer coming in tonight, deadline 7pm.

Here's what I think: this house is priced right and you've been looking for three months. I don't want to lose it on price. My recommendation is to go in at $847,000 with a 7-day option period and a strong earnest money deposit ($15,000). That positions you well without overpaying.

Can you confirm you want to move forward? I'll have the offer ready within the hour.

Diana
```
