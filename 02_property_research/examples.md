## Example 1: Pre-Showing Research Brief — Relocating Family

**Incoming handoff:**
Research brief needed for 2847 Barton Springs Road. Clients are the Garcia family — relocating buyers, pre-approved at $950k, two kids (ages 8 and 11). Showing is Thursday. Client type: relocating_family. Anxiety level: medium. Key concern: schools and commute to downtown.

---

**Research Brief**

---

**Property Research Brief**
2847 Barton Springs Road, Austin TX
May 12, 2026 | Prepared for: Garcia Family | Agent: [assigned]

---

### 1. Bottom Line Up Front

This is a strong buy for the Garcia family if the condition holds under inspection. The Barton Hills location delivers on both school quality and commute, and the property is priced at the lower end of recent comps. The agent's job Thursday is to let the family experience the neighborhood — the house will sell itself once they feel the area.

### 2. Property Overview

4/3, 2,240 sq ft, built 1978, original foundation, updated kitchen (2019), original bathrooms. Listed at $895k after one price reduction (from $925k, 22 days ago). The reduction signals some seller urgency — worth noting.

**Condition signals from listing photos:** Roof appears original or near-end-of-life. HVAC unit visible in backyard appears to be a 2015-2018 vintage. Driveway shows some cracking. Cosmetically dated bathrooms are a buyer opportunity or a negotiation point.

### 3. Neighborhood Character

Barton Hills is one of Austin's most stable legacy neighborhoods. Tree-canopied streets, walkable to Barton Springs Pool, strong neighbor community. The tradeoff is traffic on Barton Springs Road itself — the street the property fronts on is a primary arterial, and traffic noise is present during peak hours. The Garcia family should walk the front of the property during the Thursday showing around school pick-up time to experience real noise levels.

This is a "forever neighborhood" for Austin families — low turnover, strong owner-occupancy, minimal investor activity.

### 4. Market Position & Pricing Analysis

Recent comps (90 days, Barton Hills, 3/2+ above 2,000 sq ft):
- 1909 Bluebonnet Lane: $910k, 2,180 sq ft, closed 34 days ago
- 2215 Barton Skyline: $940k, 2,310 sq ft, closed 19 days ago
- 3102 Treemont: $875k, 2,100 sq ft, closed 41 days ago (needed kitchen work)

Current list at $895k with a price reduction from $925k after 22 days is slightly below the comp center. This is correctly priced — possibly 1-3% below fair market given the condition notes above.

**DOM interpretation:** 22 days with a reduction in Barton Hills signals the original price was aspirational and the seller has recalibrated. This is not a distressed seller, but it is a motivated one. The window for negotiation is real.

### 5. Hidden Risks

- **Roof:** Listing photos suggest original or near-original roof. A 1978 build with no disclosed roof replacement is a flag. Inspection should include roof assessment — this could be $15k-$25k.
- **Traffic noise:** Barton Springs Road frontage means peak-hour noise is a reality. Should be experienced during the showing, not discovered post-offer.
- **Foundation:** 1978 slab in Central Austin. Not a red flag by itself, but inspection must include foundation assessment. Piers would be disclosed; verify seller disclosure.
- **No permit history available from listing:** Request seller disclosure for any major work, additions, or permit history before offer.

### 6. Resale Outlook

Strong. Barton Hills consistently appreciates above Austin average due to location fundamentals (Barton Springs, downtown proximity, school quality). The two risks to long-term value are: continued Barton Springs Road traffic increases (urban growth trajectory is toward more, not less), and the neighborhood's age profile (many 1970s builds require ongoing infrastructure investment). For a 5-7 year hold, this is a low-risk asset.

### 7. Negotiation Leverage

**Buyer's advantage:** Price reduction signals seller recalibration. 22 days on market in a neighborhood where well-priced properties move in under 14 days is meaningful. There is room for a measured offer below list — $875k-$885k is defensible based on comps and condition notes. Roof and potential foundation costs are legitimate contingency-based negotiation points post-inspection.

**Seller's advantage:** There are no competing listings of this size and quality in Barton Hills right now. The Garcia family is unlikely to find a comparable alternative in their timeline. This limits how aggressive the offer can be.

**Recommended opening position:** $880k with standard option period and 30-day close. The seller is motivated but not desperate — an aggressive lowball creates ill will in a community-tight neighborhood where the agent will do business again.

### 8. Emotional Fit for Relocating Family

This property scores well against the Garcia family's profile:
- Schools: Barton Hills Elementary (currently rated 8/10, stable trend) → O. Henry Middle → Austin High. All solid, all walkable. This directly addresses their top concern.
- Commute: 12 minutes to downtown without traffic, 20-25 minutes at peak. Acceptable.
- Neighborhood feel: Family-oriented, strong community, kids in the area. Matches their stated desire for neighborhood stability.
- Condition: The work items (roof, bathrooms) are manageable but represent real costs. Given their relocation stress, the agent should address condition honestly and help them understand what they're getting into. Do not minimize the roof.

**Risk for this client type:** The Garcias are out-of-state buyers with a hard move-in deadline. They are susceptible to making an offer quickly due to timeline pressure. The agent should encourage them to take the full option period and not waive inspection.

### 9. Recommended Approach

Walk the family through the neighborhood strengths first — schools, walkability, community feel — and let them fall in love with where they'll be living before addressing the property specifics; then address condition items directly and position them as negotiating tools, not deal-breakers.

## Example 2: CMA for Seller — Pricing Expectation Gap

**Incoming handoff:**
Pricing analysis needed for 1847 Travis Heights Blvd. Seller is Patricia Morales, emotionally attached seller, expects $950k. Diana has spoken with Patricia and the family. Agent needs CMA context before the formal pricing conversation.

---

**Research Brief (Condensed)**

**1847 Travis Heights Blvd — Pricing Context**

**Bottom line:** Market supports $800k-$835k. The $950k expectation reflects the property's emotional significance and the family's perception of Travis Heights' appreciation. The gap is real, and this conversation requires care.

**Comp support:**
- 2103 Travis Heights Blvd: $815k, 3/2, 1,900 sq ft, sold 28 days ago
- 1622 Alta Vista: $840k, 3/2, 2,050 sq ft, sold 45 days ago
- 911 Woodlawn: $795k, 3/2, 1,850 sq ft, sold 62 days ago (needed updating)

The Morales property is approximately 1,920 sq ft per county records. No major updates disclosed. Original kitchen and baths visible in property records photos.

**Why $950k doesn't hold:** Travis Heights has appreciated significantly, and the family is correct that prices have risen. But appreciation is relative to starting point. The comps reflect the current market ceiling for properties of this type and condition in this micro-location.

**How Diana should frame the pricing conversation:**
Lead with the genuine good news — the property has appreciated meaningfully, and the family will realize a strong return. Then present the comps as the market's current ceiling, not as a ceiling imposed by the agent. Emphasize that pricing at market maximizes buyer competition and reduces days on market — both of which serve the family's interest in a clean, fast closing.

**What to avoid:** Do not present $950k as "impossible" — that creates adversarial energy. Present $815k-$835k as the range most likely to result in a fast, strong offer, and explain what pricing above that range typically means (longer time on market, price reductions, stigma).
