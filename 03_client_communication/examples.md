## Example 1: BAD vs GOOD — Same Situation, Two Client Types

**Situation:** Inspection came back. One significant item: roof needs replacement, estimated $18,000. Minor items: two outlets not grounded, HVAC filter needs replacement.

---

### Client A: First-Time Buyer (Anxious, Low Confidence)

**❌ BAD Output:**
> Subject: Inspection Results
>
> Hi Sarah,
>
> The inspection report came back. There are some items to address including a roof replacement (~$18,000), two ungrounded outlets, and an HVAC filter. Let me know how you'd like to proceed.
>
> Best,
> [Agent]

**Why this fails:**
- "$18,000 roof" with no context creates panic
- No separation of significant vs. minor items
- "Let me know how you'd like to proceed" leaves an anxious first-time buyer with no guidance
- No reassurance that this is a manageable situation
- No recommended next step

---

**✅ GOOD Output:**
> Subject: Inspection results — here's what we found and what I recommend
>
> Hi Sarah,
>
> Good news: the inspection didn't turn up anything that changes my view of this house. Here's what came back:
>
> **The one significant item:** The inspector flagged that the roof is near end of its useful life and will need replacement — estimated cost is around $18,000. This is actually pretty common in homes of this age, and it gives us a clear, documented basis to go back to the seller and ask for either a price reduction or a credit at closing to cover it.
>
> **The minor stuff:** Two outlets in the basement aren't grounded (standard fix, under $200) and the HVAC filter needs replacement (the kind you pick up at Home Depot for $30). Neither of these is a concern.
>
> **My recommendation:** Let's ask the seller for a $15,000 credit at closing. This is a reasonable ask given the documented inspection finding, and it's more likely to move forward than asking them to replace the roof themselves before closing. I'll draft the amendment request today.
>
> How does that sound? I want to make sure you're comfortable with the approach before I reach out to the listing agent.
>
> [Agent]

**Why this works:**
- Opens with reassurance ("doesn't change my view")
- Clearly separates significant from minor
- Normalizes the roof situation ("pretty common")
- Turns the finding into leverage, not a problem
- Gives a specific recommendation
- Ends with a clear action and asks for her buy-in

---

### Client B: Investor (Analytical, Numbers-First)

**✅ GOOD Output (same situation):**
> Subject: 2847 Barton Springs — Inspection Summary
>
> Inspection came back with one significant item: roof is at end of life, ~$18,000 replacement. Two minor items: ungrounded outlets ($150-200 fix) and HVAC filter (nominal).
>
> Recommendation: Push for a $15k credit at closing. Strong documentation supports the ask. The seller has already reduced once — they're motivated to close. I'll submit the amendment today unless you want to discuss.
>
> [Agent]

**Why this works:**
- Leads with the number
- No hand-holding
- Recommendation is direct and confident
- Trust the investor to make his own decision on the ask

## Example 2: Competing Offer Alert

**Client type:** Relocating family (medium anxiety, detailed communication preference)
**Situation:** 24 hours after submitting offer, listing agent calls to say another offer just came in.

**✅ GOOD Output:**
> Subject: Important update — competing offer on Barton Springs
>
> Hi Marcus and Elena,
>
> I just got off the phone with the listing agent. Another offer came in on the property about an hour ago, so we're now in a multi-offer situation.
>
> Here's what this means for you and your options:
>
> **Option 1 — Hold your current offer.** The seller may still choose yours based on terms (you're pre-approved with a fast close — that matters). This is a real possibility.
>
> **Option 2 — Strengthen your offer.** You could increase to $905k (still within your ceiling) and reduce the option period from 10 to 7 days. This signals seriousness without changing your fundamental position.
>
> **Option 3 — Walk.** If this doesn't feel right at a higher price, we find another property. There will be others, and your timeline still allows for it.
>
> My recommendation given your situation: I'd go to $902k and keep the 7-day option. You have the strongest financing in the market (pre-approved, not a contingency buyer) — that's worth more than people think to sellers who want certainty. This positions you well without overextending.
>
> I need a decision by 4pm today so I can get back to the listing agent before close of business. Can you call me in the next hour?
>
> [Agent]

## Example 3: Financing Delay Communication

**Client type:** First-time buyer, high anxiety
**Situation:** Lender requests additional documentation, pushing approval timeline back 5 days. Closing date is at risk.

**✅ GOOD Output:**
> Subject: Financing update — small delay, here's the plan
>
> Hi Sarah,
>
> I want to get ahead of something before you hear about it another way.
>
> Your lender reached out today and needs one more piece of documentation before they can issue the final commitment letter. This is going to push their timeline back by about 5 days — which puts our current closing date in a tight spot.
>
> Here's what I'm doing about it:
> - I've already reached out to the listing agent to give them a heads up and ask for a brief extension. Sellers are usually cooperative when the request comes early and with a clear reason — I'm expecting a positive response.
> - I've also followed up with your lender directly to make sure this request is being prioritized.
>
> The document your lender needs is [specific document]. Can you have that to them by tomorrow morning? The faster we get it to them, the faster they move.
>
> The deal is not in jeopardy. This kind of documentation request happens more often than people expect and it resolves quickly. I'll update you as soon as I hear back from the listing agent.
>
> [Agent]
